Learning Section 02

Cash Flow Management.

Understand when money comes in, when it goes out, and how to maintain a healthy cash position.

Step 01

Understand your cash flow

Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of your finances over a period. A positive cash flow means more money came in than went out; negative cash flow means the opposite.

Inflows

Salary, business receipts, side income, and other cash received.

Outflows

Housing, food, bills, debt payments, transfers to savings, and other cash paid.

Net cash flow

Total inflows minus total outflows over the same period.

Worked example: 35,000 inflows minus 29,000 outflows = 6,000 positive monthly cash flow.

Cash flow is different from profit

A household can earn enough over a month yet still face a cash shortage if large bills are due before payday. Timing matters as much as the monthly total.

Learning Section 03

Net Worth Improvement.

Know what you own, understand what you owe, and measure progress over time.

Step 01

What is net worth?

Net worth is the value of your assets minus your liabilities. It is a snapshot of your financial position at a particular date.

Assets

Cash, savings, investments, and property you own.

Liabilities

Outstanding loans, credit card balances, and other debts.

Net Worth

Assets − Liabilities.

Worked example: 500,000 in assets minus 180,000 in liabilities = 320,000 net worth.

A negative net worth is not a personal failure. It is a measurement that can help you identify priorities and track improvement.

Step 01

Understand the investment landscape

An investment platform is a service or channel through which people can access investment products. A platform is not the same thing as the underlying investment.

Canada: RRSP

Bank investment channels (e.g., RRSP in Canada)

May provide access to deposits and managed investment products. Compare terms, liquidity, and whether deposit protection applies to the specific product. In Canada, banks also offer the RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan), a tax-deferred retirement account.

Philippines & global

VUL and life insurance

Life insurance protects your family’s income if you die. A VUL (variable unit-linked) policy adds an investment fund to that cover, so part of its value moves with the market. Buy for protection first, and judge the investment part separately.

Canada accounts & VUL

Fund platforms (mutual funds)

A mutual fund pools many investors’ money into one managed portfolio. The RRSP, TFSA, FHSA, RESP or VUL policy is the wrapper that sets the tax or insurance rules; the fund inside sets the risk and return. Compare objectives, fees, holdings and redemption rules.

Where funds sit

An RRSP can hold mutual funds. Growth inside the account is tax-deferred, and withdrawals are taxed as income.1

A TFSA can hold mutual funds. Growth and withdrawals are tax-free, so a TFSA left only in cash gives up most of its advantage.1

An FHSA can generally hold the same investments as an RRSP and a TFSA, including mutual funds. Match the fund’s risk to when you plan to buy.1

An RESP can hold mutual funds too, and the government grant is invested alongside your contributions. The closer the child is to post-secondary school, the less time a fund has to recover from a fall.1

RRSP: Registered Retirement Savings Plan

  • Each year you earn new room equal to % of last year’s earned income, up to the annual dollar limit: C$ for , with C$ announced for . Unused room carries forward.910
  • Tax benefit now: contributions reduce your taxable income. Contributions made in the first 60 days of a year can count for the previous tax year.10
  • Tax-deferred growth: nothing is taxed inside the plan. Withdrawals are taxed as income, with tax withheld at source: %, % or % outside Quebec, depending on the amount.12
  • At : by the end of that year you must convert the RRSP to a RRIF, buy an annuity, or withdraw it. RRIF minimum withdrawals have no withholding tax.12
  • Non-residents: RRSP withdrawals made while living outside Canada face a flat % withholding tax.8

RRSP withdrawals at a glance

Money can leave an RRSP in four ways. Two of them, the Home Buyers’ Plan and the Lifelong Learning Plan, let you borrow from yourself tax-free, as long as you repay on schedule.

Home Buyers’ Plan (HBP)

Withdraw up to C$ tax-free to buy or build a qualifying first home, then repay it over years.1516

Insight It’s an interest-free loan from your future self. Every year it’s out, that money isn’t growing for retirement.

Lifelong Learning Plan (LLP)

Withdraw up to C$ a year, and C$ in total, for your or your spouse’s education or training. Repay at least % a year, within years.17

Insight Useful for a career change or upgrade, but plan the repayments into your budget before you enrol.

General withdrawals

Taking money out for any other reason adds the amount to your taxable income for the year, and tax is withheld at source.128

Insight Treat it as a last resort. Use your emergency fund or TFSA first.

Tax implications

Withholding (%, % or % outside Quebec; % for non-residents) is only a prepayment, so your final tax depends on your total income. The contribution room you used is not restored when you withdraw.12818

Insight Unlike a TFSA, RRSP room is gone for good once used. Only HBP and LLP repayments go back in without using new room.

Step 01 · Spotlight

VUL and life insurance

Life insurance replaces your income for your family if you die. A variable unit-linked (VUL) policy combines that cover with an investment fund in one contract, so it is both protection and an investment, and should be judged as both.

How your premium works in a VUL

Premium you pay
Insurance & policy chargespay for your life cover
Units in your chosen fundbond, equity, balanced, money market32
Fund value = units × NAVPUrises and falls with the market233
  • In the Philippines, the Insurance Commission treats a VUL as a single life insurance contract, not as separate insurance and investment products.34
  • Premiums for the investment part go into a separate fund. Your fund value equals your number of units times the net asset value per unit (NAVPU).2
  • The cover itself carries no investment risk, but the fund’s returns are not guaranteed and money invested can be lost, because it depends on market performance.33
  • Rules under review: in the Insurance Commission asked for comments on draft revised guidelines for VUL policies.35

Potential advantages

  • Life cover and long-term investing in a single policy
  • Builds a regular saving habit through fixed premiums
  • Choice of funds to match your risk appetite
  • A death benefit for your beneficiaries

Things to weigh

  • Charges reduce how much is invested, often most in the early years
  • If the fund falls, you may need to pay more to keep the cover in force
  • Surrendering early can return less than you paid in
  • Harder to compare than term insurance plus a separate fund
Protection first: decide how much cover your family needs before looking at fund returns. Ask for the policy’s charges in writing, and compare the VUL with buying term life insurance and investing the difference in a low-cost fund.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • How much of each premium goes to charges in years 1–5?
  • What is the guaranteed death benefit, and does it depend on the fund value?
  • What happens to my cover if the fund value falls?
  • What are the surrender charges, and for how many years do they apply?
  • Are the insurer and the agent licensed by the Insurance Commission?

Real estate investment property

Property can produce rental income and grow in value, but it ties up a lot of money in one asset. Weigh it against your emergency fund, debts and other goals first.

What you actually earn

Rental income+Price growth−Taxes & fees−Maintenance & dues−Vacancy−Financing cost=Your real return

Pros

  • A tangible asset you can live in or rent out
  • Potential rental income and long-term price growth
  • A mortgage lets you control a larger asset (leverage)
  • Can protect you against rising rents

Cons

  • Large upfront cost, plus transaction taxes and fees
  • Illiquid: selling can take months
  • Ongoing costs: maintenance, dues, insurance, vacancies
  • Much of your wealth tied to one location
  • Ownership rules for foreigners vary by country

Philippines

  • The 1987 Constitution bars foreign nationals from owning land.36
  • Foreigners may own condominium units, provided foreign ownership in the project stays within % (Condominium Act, RA 4726).3736
  • Former natural-born Filipinos can own limited residential land: up to m² of urban land or one hectare of rural land (RA 8179).38
  • Selling property held as a capital asset triggers a % final capital gains tax on the selling price or fair market value, whichever is higher.39 Example: sold for ₱ with a fair market value of ₱ → % × ₱ = ₱.

Canada

  • Profit from selling your principal residence is generally tax-free if it was your main home for every year you owned it.40
  • Gains on a rental or second property are taxable. As of , % of a capital gain is included in taxable income.41
  • Non-Canadians (people who are neither citizens nor permanent residents) are barred from buying most residential property in urban areas until .42
  • Proposed change: Budget 2026 () proposes extending the ban on existing homes to , while exempting new homes. This is a proposal, not yet law.43

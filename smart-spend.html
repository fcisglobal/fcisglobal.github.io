Learning Section 02

Cash Flow Management.

Understand when money comes in, when it goes out, and how to maintain a healthy cash position.

1. Understand your cash flow

Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of your finances over a period. A positive cash flow means more money came in than went out; negative cash flow means the opposite.

Inflows

Salary, business receipts, side income, and other cash received.

Outflows

Housing, food, bills, debt payments, transfers to savings, and other cash paid.

Net cash flow

Total inflows minus total outflows over the same period.

Worked example: ₱35,000 inflows minus ₱29,000 outflows = ₱6,000 positive monthly cash flow.

Cash flow is different from profit

A household can earn enough over a month yet still face a cash shortage if large bills are due before payday. Timing matters as much as the monthly total.

2. Build a monthly cash flow statement

Use the calculator to compare inflows and outflows. Savings transfers are counted as cash leaving your available spending account; they are not necessarily an economic loss.

3. Manage payment timing

List your expected paydays and bill due dates. A positive monthly cash flow does not automatically mean you have enough cash on every day of the month.

Practical cash flow habits

Keep a bill calendar, maintain a cash buffer, review recurring charges, and prepare for irregular expenses.

Plan for irregular costs

For an annual ₱12,000 expense, setting aside ₱1,000 each month can reduce pressure when payment becomes due.

Learning Section 03

Net Worth Improvement.

Know what you own, understand what you owe, and measure progress over time.

1. What is net worth?

Net worth is the value of your assets minus your liabilities. It is a snapshot of your financial position at a particular date.

Assets

Cash, savings, investments, and property you own.

Liabilities

Outstanding loans, credit card balances, and other debts.

Net Worth

Assets − Liabilities.

Worked example: ₱500,000 in assets minus ₱180,000 in liabilities = ₱320,000 net worth.

A negative net worth is not a personal failure. It is a measurement that can help you identify priorities and track improvement.

Learning Section 04

Investment Platforms.

Learn how different investment channels work, what fees can cost, and how to assess risk before investing.

1. Understand the investment landscape

An investment platform is a service or channel through which people can access investment products. A platform is not the same thing as the underlying investment.

Bank investment channels

May provide access to deposits and managed investment products. Compare terms, liquidity, and whether deposit protection applies to the specific product.

Brokerage platforms

May provide access to shares, ETFs, and other securities. Understand trading fees, custody arrangements, and market risk.

Fund platforms

May offer mutual funds or pooled investments. Compare objectives, management fees, holdings, and redemption rules.

Digital investing apps

Can simplify access but still require checks on regulation, fees, security, and the actual products being offered.

Key distinction: a familiar app interface does not guarantee that an investment is suitable, safe, or protected against loss.

2. Compare products, risks, and costs

Investment typeMain considerationPotential risk
Bank depositsLiquidity, rate, product termsInflation and institution risk; applicable protection varies
BondsIssuer, maturity, yieldCredit and interest-rate risk

High-risk speculative assetsSuitability, transparency, custodyPotentially severe or total loss

Examples are educational categories, not recommendations to purchase any product.

Why diversification matters

Diversification spreads exposure across assets rather than concentrating everything in one investment. It can help manage some risks, but it cannot eliminate losses.

3. Understand the impact of platform fees

Small recurring fees can make a meaningful difference over long periods. Compare all-in costs, not just headline platform charges.
